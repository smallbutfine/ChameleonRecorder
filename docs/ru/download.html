How to Flash with Raspberry Pi Imager

  1. Download the image: Download the latest daw-image.img.xz from the Releases page
  2. Open Raspberry Pi Imager: Download and launch the official Raspberry Pi Imager on your Windows, macOS, or Linux computer
  3. Choose Device: Click Choose Device and select Raspberry Pi 4
  4. Choose OS: Click Choose OS, scroll down to the bottom, select Use custom, and pick the downloaded daw-image.img.xz file
  5. Choose Storage: Insert your microSD card (16 GB or larger) and select it under Choose Storage
  6. Optional OS Customisation: Click Next and choose Edit Settings if you want to pre-configure your home/studio Wi-Fi credentials, set your Wi-Fi country code, or add an SSH password/key for the recorder user. If you skip this step (click No), the Pi will automatically start its standalone ChameleonRecorder Wi-Fi hotspot on first boot
  7. Write the Image: Click Yes to confirm and wait for Raspberry Pi Imager to finish writing and verifying the SD card
  8. Boot & Record: Insert the microSD card into your Raspberry Pi 4B, connect your USB flash drive and USB audio interface(s), and power it on

First Boot

  1. Insert the microSD card into the Raspberry Pi 4B
  2. Connect a USB flash drive (exFAT or ext4) to a blue USB 3.0 port
  3. Connect your USB audio interface(s)
  4. Power on the Pi
  5. Wait ~25 seconds — if no known Wi-Fi is found, the ChameleonRecorder hotspot appears
  6. Connect to the hotspot and open http://10.42.0.1:8080

Fallback Hotspot vs. Home Wi-Fi

ChameleonRecorder supports two connection modes:

ModeWhen it activatesURL
Home / Studio Wi-FiIf you pre-configured Wi-Fi in Raspberry Pi Imager, or if the Pi has previously connected to a known networkhttp://<PI_IP>:8080 or http://DAW.local:8080
Fallback HotspotIf no known Wi-Fi is found within 25 seconds of boothttp://10.42.0.1:8080 (SSID: ChameleonRecorder, Password: chameleon123)
Tip: The fallback hotspot is perfect for stage and rehearsal use — no router needed. Just connect your phone or laptop directly to the Pi's Wi-Fi.
